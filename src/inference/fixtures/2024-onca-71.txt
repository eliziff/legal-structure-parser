(1.1) A prosecutor shall not commence or continue a prosecution against a person who is the subject of the order unless, in the opinion of the prosecutor,

REASONS FOR DECISION
[1] This is an appeal from conviction for sexual assault. The sole ground of appeal rests on a suggestion that the trial judge applied uneven scrutiny to the evidence of the appellant and complainant. This is a notoriously difficult allegation to make out on appeal, largely owing to the high degree of deference that appellate courts owe to trial judges’ findings of credibility. Despite Mr. Ertel’s capable submissions, this appeal fails.
[2] While there were difficulties with both the appellant’s and complainant’s evidence, the trial judge carefully reviewed the inconsistencies and explained in detail why he rejected the appellant’s credibility and why he nonetheless accepted the complainant’s credibility.
[3] The trial judge did not apply a different standard to the appellant’s and complainant’s evidence. Once he rejected the appellant’s evidence for the detailed reasons given and found that his evidence did not raise a reasonable doubt, this case was left to be decided on the third prong of R. v. W.(D.), [1991] 1 S.C.R. 742. The trial judge gave cogent reasons for accepting the complainant’s credibility. This included relying on the fact that, unlike the appellant, the complainant was not inconsistent on the core elements of the offence.
[4] While the appellant argues that the trial judge erroneously ignored a number of the inconsistencies in the complainant’s evidence because he described them as immaterial or irrelevant, we do not read the reasons in this way. Read in context, the trial judge was merely using these characterizations to reference the fact that those inconsistencies did not go to the core essence of the allegations.
[5] In addition, the trial judge noted that, while legally unnecessary, there was corroboration of the complainant’s evidence, as elicited through the complainant’s roommates and medical evidence, that supported the complainant’s credibility and reliability. It was open to the trial judge to come to the conclusions he reached. It is not for this court to retry the case in circumstances where we owe deference to the trial judge’s findings.
[6] The appeal is dismissed.
